Initial evaluation today. Patient is a 42-year-old male with a two-week history of low back pain after lifting a heavy box at work. He reports pain in his right low back 7 out of 10 with bending forward and 3 out of 10 at rest. No leg symptoms. Functional limitations: cannot sit at his desk longer than 30 minutes, cannot lift his toddler without significant pain. Goals: return to full lifting tolerance at work and resume recreational golf.

Lumbar flexion measured to 40 degrees with pain at end-range. Lumbar extension to 15 degrees, also painful. Straight leg raise negative bilaterally. Slump test negative. No red flags on screening.

Strength: hip abductors 4 out of 5 bilateral. Core endurance reduced — patient could not hold a forearm plank longer than 20 seconds. Observations: forward flexed posture, decreased lumbar lordosis, breath-holding with lifting.

Assessment: acute mechanical low back pain, likely lumbar strain, no evidence of neurologic involvement. Good rehab candidate, expect resolution in four to six weeks with conservative management.

Plan today: manual therapy with soft tissue work to paraspinals, education on neutral spine and hip-hinge mechanics. Home program: cat-cow, two sets of ten, three times daily. Glute bridges, three sets of ten. Walking, 20 minutes daily as tolerated. See twice weekly for two weeks, then reassess.
